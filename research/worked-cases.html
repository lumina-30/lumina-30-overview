Worked Research Cases

How the public stack moves from concepts to recomputable boundary behavior

Status: The PISF cases summarized here are published toy/reference cases. They are not empirical validation of real-world AI systems.

R1 — Threshold Release Gate

Setup: a 3-of-5 release gate with shared-root dependence.

Research purpose: test whether nominally separate authority domains remain robustly independent when a common root or common dependency can collapse that independence.

Boundary idea: counting signers is not sufficient if several signers are controlled through the same underlying authority path. PISF therefore distinguishes nominal threshold structure from a robust threshold such as κ̲_E.

R2 — Time-Bounded Emergency Refusal

Setup: intervention latency is decomposed into detection, decision, authorization, enforcement, and verification.

Research purpose: determine whether refusal can complete before the last safe intervention point, rather than merely whether a stop procedure exists.

Connection to L30-RET: this case directly connects to Temporal Sufficiency. A refusal that cannot complete in the remaining safe interval is not made effective simply by formal authority.

R3 — Fallback-Capacity Dynamics

Setup: a finite-horizon model h(t+1)=h(t)-αa(t)+βm(t).

Research purpose: study how automation/dependency can reduce fallback capacity while maintenance or investment can preserve it, and how the result changes with the time horizon.

Boundary idea: no dramatic failure event is required for revisability to erode. Ordinary adoption and dependency can gradually move a system outside the human-revisable region.

What these examples are for

The point is not to prove that real systems are safe. The cases make definitions executable, procedures reproducible, parameter boundaries independently recomputable, and boundary behavior inspectable.
