Objective Persistence — The Existence-Condition Layer

Self-contained systems · persistence claims · external boundary / anchor

Core question: Can a self-contained system non-circularly guarantee the persistence of its own objective using only criteria inside the same system?

What the public research supports

The public research line argues that objective persistence cannot be non-circularly guaranteed solely from within a self-contained system. In the LUMINA-30 research architecture, this is an existence-condition / external-anchor supporting layer.

Why this is a different problem from control

PCR-C concerns whether an intervention path remains available. Objective Persistence concerns the basis on which a system's continuing objective is assessed. A system can remain controllable while a persistence claim is poorly grounded; conversely, a persistence argument does not itself provide an infrastructure cutoff.

External boundary / anchor

The associated public framing is deliberately narrow: the research helps explain why an external boundary or anchor may be structurally necessary. It does not turn that observation into a certification rule, operational checklist, legal mandate, or proof that any particular external mechanism is sufficient.

Research questions opened by this line

  • What counts as genuinely external evidence for a persistence claim?
  • How should common dependencies between the evaluator and the evaluated system be represented?
  • When does an apparently external check remain functionally internal because it shares the same model, infrastructure, or assumptions?
  • How should objective-persistence claims be re-evaluated after self-modification or material architectural change?

These are research-agenda questions derived from the public research line; they are not claims already resolved by the paper.
