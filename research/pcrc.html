PCR-C — Control Before the Boundary

Pre-Critical Recursive Cutoff · infrastructure/control research layer

Core question: How can infrastructure-level control act before irreversibility risk becomes dominant and intervention capacity is exceeded?

Research object

PCR-C is a staged infrastructure control framework. Its role is not to redefine the LUMINA-30 boundary, but to formalize one infrastructure-layer mechanism for acting before that boundary is crossed.

Why a control path can fail before a stop is requested

A nominal stop control is not the same as an effective intervention path. The relevant research problem is whether intervention remains structurally reachable before recursive or self-reinforcing escalation makes refusal ineffective.

Relationship to LUMINA-30

LUMINA-30 asks the boundary question: was effective human refusal still available before irreversible impact? PCR-C addresses an infrastructure-layer question: can control act before the system reaches the point at which that refusal becomes structurally ineffective?

Role separation: LUMINA-30 defines the boundary question. PCR-C supplies a staged infrastructure-layer cutoff model. Neither should be collapsed into the other.

Research use

  • Reasoning about staged intervention before irreversibility thresholds.
  • Separating formal stop authority from the continued reachability of the infrastructure control path.
  • Connecting pre-incident review to infrastructure-level control design.
  • Studying where intervention capacity becomes insufficient relative to system escalation.

What PCR-C does not claim

PCR-C does not replace incident review, human responsibility, the LUMINA-30 canonical boundary definition, or every other AI safety method. It is one research layer in the public stack.
