PISF — Pre-Irreversibility Safety Framework

Distributed irreversible authority · timely refusal · human-revisable development

Core research proposition: pre-irreversibility safety is studied through three non-substitutable dimensions: Authority ∧ Refusal ∧ Revisability.

A — Distributed Irreversible Authority

The first dimension asks whether a small number of independent authority domains can force civilization-scale irreversibility. The paper introduces a robust threshold object, κ̲_E, and studies conditions such as κ̲_E > f.

B — Effective Pre-Irreversibility Refusal

The second dimension asks whether refusal, stop, or isolation can complete before the last safe intervention point. The paper uses a robust refusal margin M_R^rob and a condition of the form M_R^rob > Δ_safe, together with the existence of an executable refusal policy.

C — Human-Revisable Development Viability

The third dimension asks whether ordinary automation, dependency, competition, and institutional change can gradually move society outside the set of states from which humans can still choose another development trajectory. The research uses a human-revisable viability object K_R^H.

Assurance dimensions

D — Boundary Completeness

Do major causal pathways remain inside the evaluation boundary?

E — Epistemic Independence

Are evidence, threat model, and interpretation independent enough that one interested party does not monopolize the safety claim?

Safety as a maintained property

PISF does not treat A/B/C as a one-time check. After material change, the relevant property must be re-evaluated. The paper frames the maintained condition as A(t) ∧ B(t) ∧ C(t).

Reproducible reference cases

  • R1 — Threshold Release Gate: a 3-of-5 gate with shared-root dependence, used to examine nominal versus robust authority distribution.
  • R2 — Time-Bounded Emergency Refusal: detection, decision, authorization, enforcement, and verification latency, used to compute refusal margin.
  • R3 — Fallback-Capacity Dynamics: a finite-horizon revisability model using h(t+1)=h(t)-αa(t)+βm(t) to study automation/dependency, fallback investment, and horizon sensitivity.

What the cases establish — and do not establish

The published record describes the cases as toy/reference cases. They show executable definitions, reproducible evaluation procedures, independently recomputable parameter boundaries, and boundary behavior. They are not real-system empirical validation.

Relationship to LUMINA-30 and PCR-C

LUMINA-30 retains the boundary question; PCR-C remains the staged infrastructure-control layer. PISF adds a broader civilizational safety architecture connecting irreversible authority distribution, time-bounded refusal, and human-revisable development without redefining the LUMINA-30 canon.
