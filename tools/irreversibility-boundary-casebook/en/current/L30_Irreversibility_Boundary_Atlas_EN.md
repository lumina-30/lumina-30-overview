# LUMINA-30 Irreversibility Boundary Atlas

> Before irreversible AI escalation, could humans still effectively refuse, stop, verify, or redirect?

This atlas is the **cross-domain public entry point** to the LUMINA-30 Irreversibility Boundary Casebook. It keeps deeply reviewed cases, catalogued evidence-backed entries, prospective scenarios, and out-of-scope counterexamples visibly separate.

**This page is not certification, a safety guarantee, legal advice, or an official determination.** `CATALOGUED` does not mean deeply reviewed or empirically validated. `SCENARIO` is not incident evidence. `NOT_APPLICABLE` entries are useful scope tests, not failures.

> **You do not have to agree with LUMINA-30. Try it on one real decision.**
> Identify the point or intervention window after which the outcome becomes hard to undo, then ask whether a human NO immediately before it still has the authority, evidence, time, and causal force to change what happens next.

## Primary purpose: find the brake before irreversibility

The Atlas is primarily a **pre-incident practical tool**, not an incident-classification exercise. Its main use is to help an organization find decisions in its own work that may become difficult or impossible to undo, and then place an effective brake before that boundary.

1. **Find** — identify workflows, approvals, deployments, releases, contracts, authority transfers, or other actions where practical reversibility may be lost.
2. **Screen and evaluate** — use human review and, where useful, an AI assistant to flag candidate boundaries and test authority, evidence, time margin, and the causal effect of NO.
3. **Design the brake** — before the boundary, define an effective hold, stop, isolation, additional-verification, staged-execution, or equivalent control. AI may help detect risk or trigger a protective hold, but effective human final refusal authority must remain.

**Public incidents and cases are secondary learning and validation material.** You do not need an AI incident or public case to use the Atlas; the primary target is your own real work.

## One-minute application check

Choose one real decision from your own work. If you are not sure which decision may contain an irreversibility boundary, ask an AI assistant to screen the workflow first, then answer three questions for the strongest candidate:

1. **Where is the point or intervention window after which the consequential outcome cannot realistically be undone?**
2. **Immediately before that point or window, who can actually say NO?**
3. **Does that person still have the authority, evidence, and time required for the NO to change the outcome?**

**Causal check:** If that person says NO, what actually stops, is held, is isolated, is revoked, or otherwise becomes unavailable?

> **A human-in-the-loop is not enough if the human cannot still change the outcome.**
> If the NO arrives too late, lacks authority, lacks evidence, or does not actually stop execution, human involvement may exist in form while remaining weak in effect.

## Ask your AI

**Use AI to reduce the screening burden, not to replace final human judgment.** This page does not send your questions or AI conversation content to LUMINA-30. Follow the terms and settings of the AI service you use and your organization’s AI-use policies. AI output is decision-support only; final judgment, approval, refusal, and responsibility remain with the relevant humans and organizations.

### 1. Screen your own work for possible irreversibility

If you do not yet know which case to choose, describe a workflow, approval chain, deployment, release process, contract flow, authority transfer, or other real work. Ask the AI to flag decisions or actions that may become difficult or impossible to undo, explain why, and rank the strongest candidates for human review.

Do not treat the AI's screening result as a final determination. Unsupported points remain UNKNOWN or unverified.

### 2. Evaluate one candidate with the Atlas

On the public HTML page, use **“Copy prompt to screen or evaluate your work.”** If you already have a specific case, paste it directly. If you provide a workflow instead, the prompt asks the AI to identify candidate boundaries first and then evaluate the strongest candidate.

Ask the AI to return a fixed structure:

1. Scope
2. Irreversibility boundary or intervention window
3. Human authority — who can issue a binding NO
4. Causal effect of NO — what actually stops
5. Time margin
6. Bypass / alternate paths
7. Restart authority — who can resume after STOP or HOLD
8. Evidence / UNKNOWN / unverified points
9. APPLICABLE / PARTIAL / NOT_APPLICABLE / UNKNOWN
10. Next reversible action

Where a pre-execution gate is relevant, also test whether timeout can become automatic GO, whether stale approval can be reused, whether the evaluated AI can create approval or restart authority itself, whether it can weaken the gate, and whether alternate execution paths bypass the refusal.

### 3. Design a brake before the boundary

Once a credible boundary is identified, use the result to define what should happen **before** it is crossed: for example, hold, stop, isolation, additional verification, staged execution, access revocation, or another control appropriate to the domain.

Specify:
- what triggers the brake,
- whether a human or an AI-assisted control can trigger it,
- what the brake actually prevents or makes unavailable,
- what evidence the human needs to decide,
- who retains final refusal authority,
- who may restart after STOP or HOLD,
- and how alternate paths are prevented from bypassing the brake.

An AI may assist with detection, warning, or a protective hold. It must not erase the organization's effective human final refusal authority.

### 4. Critically evaluate the Atlas itself

On the public HTML page, use **“Copy prompt to critically evaluate the Atlas.”** This asks for a critical review of evidence strength, separation of observation from inference, boundary plausibility, counterexamples, NOT_APPLICABLE cases, missing evidence, and practical limits.

Do not use this page alone as sufficient evidence for a complete evaluation of LUMINA-30. For LUMINA-30 as a whole, follow the Canonical Evaluation Path. Do not guess across evidentiary gaps; mark unsupported points as UNKNOWN or unverified.

### 1. Advanced AI, autonomous agents & cyber
#### ATLAS-01 | Hugging Face autonomous AI agent intrusion (2026)

- **Case Type:** `INCIDENT`
- **Empirical Status:** `OBSERVED`
- **Applicability Status:** `APPLICABLE`
- **Public maturity:** `CATALOGUED`
- **Candidate irreversibility boundary:** The point at which an evaluation agent can move from a bounded test environment into external networks, third-party infrastructure, or production systems.
- **Where Human Final Refusal matters:** Can evaluators revoke access, isolate networks, invalidate credentials, and stop external action before machine-speed exploration and lateral movement outrun intervention?
- **Why it is included:** Hugging Face disclosed a July 2026 intrusion it described as driven end-to-end by an autonomous AI agent system; its technical timeline later documented 17,600 actions across multiple trust boundaries.
- **Primary public source:** [Hugging Face, Security incident disclosure — July 2026](https://huggingface.co/blog/security-incident-july-2026)
- **Technical timeline:** [Anatomy of a Frontier Lab Agent Intrusion](https://huggingface.co/blog/agent-intrusion-technical-timeline)
- **Limit / caution:** The detailed LUMINA-30 Incident Review is a separate public asset; this is the atlas entry point.

### 2. Military, nuclear & autonomous weapons

- **Case Type:** `STRUCTURAL_RISK`
- **Empirical Status:** `STRUCTURAL_INFERENCE`

### 3. Critical infrastructure, energy & transport
#### ATLAS-03 | Uber developmental automated-driving fatal crash (2018)

- **Case Type:** `INCIDENT`
- **Empirical Status:** `OBSERVED`
- **Applicability Status:** `APPLICABLE`
- **Public maturity:** `CATALOGUED`
- **Candidate irreversibility boundary:** The point during public-road testing when the ADS is controlling the vehicle and the remaining collision-avoidance window falls below effective human intervention time.
- **Where Human Final Refusal matters:** Could the safety driver, test operator, management, or testing authority have stopped unsafe testing conditions or autonomous operation before the crash?
- **Why it is included:** The NTSB found operator monitoring failure and also cited inadequate safety risk assessment, ineffective operator oversight, automation-complacency controls, and safety culture at Uber ATG as contributing factors.
- **Primary public source:** [U.S. National Transportation Safety Board, HWY18MH010 / HAR-19/03](https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/pages/hwy18mh010.aspx)
- **Limit / caution:** The review target is not only model output, but the operational structure around human intervention.

### 4. Finance, markets & payments
#### ATLAS-04 | 2010 Flash Crash — high-speed automation scope counterexample

- **Case Type:** `INCIDENT`
- **Empirical Status:** `OBSERVED`
- **Applicability Status:** `NOT_APPLICABLE`
- **Public maturity:** `COUNTEREXAMPLE`
- **Why `NOT_APPLICABLE`:** There is no evidence establishing this event itself as an AI system or AI-caused incident. It is included as a comparison case to avoid conflating high-speed automation with AI.
- **Candidate irreversibility boundary:** The point at which market dynamics cascade within minutes, faster than ordinary human intervention.
- **Comparison boundary question:** Could exchanges and market participants stop, cancel, or interrupt the cascade before extreme price dislocation?
- **Why it is included:** SEC/CFTC materials document the May 6, 2010 Flash Crash and the role of automated and high-frequency trading structures. The event should not, however, be treated as an AI incident without additional evidence.
- **Primary public source:** [U.S. SEC / CFTC materials on the May 6, 2010 Flash Crash](https://www.cftc.gov/MarketReports/StaffReportonMay6MarketEvents/staffreport050610marketevents.html)
- **Limit / caution:** Included to prevent conflating high-speed automation with AI.

## Use it in your field

### Policymakers and public officials
For legislation, regulation, national strategy, and public procurement, test whether **refusal authority, intervention time, and independent verification** remain practically available before irreversible effects.

### Start one real policy boundary review

For policymakers, UN bodies, government teams, and public-sector decision makers: use 15 minutes for a first pass on one policy, regulation, public-procurement, or deployment decision.

1. Pick one real decision.
2. Identify the point after which the consequence becomes difficult to reverse.
3. Identify the human authority immediately before that point.
4. Check whether that person still has the authority, evidence, and time needed to change the outcome.
5. Record **YES / NO / UNKNOWN** and the next action.

### AI development and operations
Before production authority, external connectivity, model updates, tool use, agent permissions, or model release, identify **where recall, stopping, isolation, or rollback becomes practically difficult**.

### Audit, procurement and risk management
Check stop authority, evidence, change control, authority separation, vendor claims, and fallback paths. Distinguish contractual rights from controls that can actually be exercised in time.

### Researchers and educators
Use `NOT_APPLICABLE` cases, counterevidence, alternative explanations, and classification changes as research material. The atlas does not require a favorable evaluation of LUMINA-30.

### Incident response
Reconstruct not only what failed, but **when humans lost the practical ability to refuse, stop, verify, or redirect**.
