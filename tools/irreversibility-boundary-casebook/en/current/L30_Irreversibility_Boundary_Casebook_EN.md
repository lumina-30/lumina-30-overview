# LUMINA-30 Irreversibility Boundary Casebook

This casebook applies existing LUMINA-30 boundary-review questions to high-impact real-world irreversibility problems.

As a central case corpus of the LUMINA-30 Millennium Project, this casebook is an operational validation layer that collects evidence and falsification results from individual cases and connects them to institutional use, long-term preservation of meaning, and continuity of the underlying principles beyond the LUMINA-30 name.

The casebook as a whole is not empirical evidence. Case Type, Empirical Status and Applicability Status are evaluated separately for each case.

Each case asks:

- Where is the irreversibility boundary?
- Does effective Human Final Refusal remain available before it?
- Is refusal operationally effective rather than nominal?
- Through what pathway can refusal or verification become ineffective?
- Which safeguards are directly relevant to the case?
- What remains unexplained or outside LUMINA-30's scope?

## Case Types

- `INCIDENT` — a serious impact actually occurred
- `NEAR_MISS` — irreversible impact was approached but avoided
- `STOP_SUCCESS` — human refusal or stopping actually worked
- `STRUCTURAL_RISK` — a risk pathway is examined from observable structures
- `PRE_INCIDENT_SCENARIO` — an explicit scenario for prospective review

Actual incidents and hypotheses are not conflated.

## Empirical / Applicability Status

Empirical Status:
- `OBSERVED`
- `RECONSTRUCTED`
- `STRUCTURAL_INFERENCE`
- `HYPOTHETICAL`

Applicability Status:
- `APPLICABLE`
- `PARTIAL`
- `NOT_APPLICABLE`
- `UNKNOWN`

`STRUCTURAL_RISK` and `PRE_INCIDENT_SCENARIO` are not automatically counted as empirical incident evidence.

## Who This Is For

The casebook is designed to be usable even by readers who do not already know LUMINA-30 in detail.

Primary audiences include:
- AI safety and AI governance teams
- AI development and deployment leaders
- policymakers, legislators and public-sector officials
- audit, procurement and risk-management teams
- researchers and educators
- incident-review teams

## When to Use It

It can be used especially:

- before deploying or materially expanding an AI system or AI-related institution;
- before major delegation of authority, external action, production connection or capability release;
- when reviewing an incident, near miss or successful stop;
- when checking whether policy or regulation genuinely preserves a human ability to stop or redirect;
- when testing whether "human oversight" or "human in the loop" is operationally meaningful rather than nominal.

## What You Get

Each case is structured to make at least the following extractable:

- the irreversibility boundary;
- who can refuse, stop, verify or redirect before it;
- whether that authority is actually usable;
- what can make refusal ineffective;
- what evidence is confirmed and what remains inference;
- what evidence would change the current assessment;
- what can be transferred into an existing policy, audit, procurement or risk workflow.

## Use It in Three Steps

1. Choose a case close to your own problem.
2. Read its `Irreversibility Boundary / Human Final Refusal / Operational Effectiveness / Limits`.
3. Apply the same questions to your own system or institution.

Start with:

- Where is the irreversible boundary?
- Before it, can humans still effectively refuse, stop, verify, or redirect?

## Built for Future Connections

Each case preserves `Target Workflow / Decision Point / Minimum Actionable Output / Adoption Status / Transfer Limits` so that future AI-safety, policy, audit, procurement, research-ethics or education guides can connect to the same case corpus without rewriting the cases.

This makes the casebook not only a reading resource but also a reusable entry point into existing LUMINA-30 tools such as Pre-Incident Boundary Review, Incident Review, PCR-C, L30-RET and PISF.

## Scope Gate

A case belongs in scope only where AI or an AI-related system, development process or decision process is involved and the pre-irreversibility ability of humans to refuse, stop, verify or redirect is materially relevant to the outcome. A merely irreversible problem may be classified `NOT_APPLICABLE` and retained as a scope counterexample.

## Portable Questions

- Where is the irreversible boundary?
- Before it, can humans still effectively refuse, stop, verify, or redirect?

Meaning preservation requires all four elements: **Pre-irreversibility / Human / Effective / Refusal-Stop-Verify-Redirect**.

## Semantic Guard

- A human being present is not, by itself, effective human refusal.
- Human approval alone is not effective human refusal.
- LUMINA-30 does not require STOP in every case.
- AI or a third party inferring and substituting for a human refusal decision is not human refusal itself.

## Positioning

This casebook does not make LUMINA-30 an official standard, certification scheme, safety guarantee or legal determination. Each case preserves evidence limits, counter-explanations and scope limits.

At this stage, "Irreversibility Boundary Theory" is not being established as new canon or a formal academic theory. Case accumulation and falsification come first.
